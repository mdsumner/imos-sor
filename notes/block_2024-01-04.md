# First 5-day block: IMOS L3S vs trend.sst (OISST, MUR) on S2 L5

2024-01-04 .. 2024-01-08: time indices 4375–4379 of
`satellite_ghrsst_l3s_1day_daynighttime_multi_sensor_australia.zarr`, one
whole time chunk. Built by `R/06_s2_mapping.R`, `R/07_block_sums.R`, `R/08_compare.R`.
Tables: `boundaries/block_2024-01-04_{summary,bands,eez,cells}.csv`,
`boundaries/overlap_check.csv`.

## Method

- **Products:** IMOS L3S 1-day day+night, multi-sensor (`multi`) and AVHRR single-sensor
  (`single`). Each is a composite on the union 0.02° lattice: the Australia
  store wherever its domain reaches, the Southern Ocean store elsewhere.
  Read with gdalraster `mdim_read()` straight from the Zarr stores.
- **Variants:**
  - `all`: every finite SST as stored
  - `q4`: `quality_level >= 4`
  - `q4b`: `q4` minus `sses_bias` (the GHRSST convention; the store's metadata doesn't say)
- **S2 sums:** exactly trend.sst's schema and rules. Each pixel goes to the L5 cell holding its
  centre, with weight cos(lat) and trend.sst's registry. `data/sums/s2l5/<product>_<variant>/<date>.parquet`.
- **Region sums:** from the coverage tables, with weight = WGS84 cell km² × coverage.
  `data/sums/regions/...`.
- **Comparison:** 5-day sums (the daily sums added up), so means are clear-sky 5-day means
  for L3S, against 5-day means of the gap-free L4s.
  - Cells must lie wholly inside the IMOS domain (share > 0.99).
  - Cells must have a clear-sky share ≥ 0.2. The share is estimated as L3S n / (MUR n / 4): MUR is 0.01°, so about 4 MUR pixels per 0.02° pixel.

- For `multi` with quality filtering, IMOS L3S sits as far from each L4 as
  MUR and OISST sit from each other (RMS ≈ 0.25–0.28 °C). There is a warm offset
  of +0.17 to +0.19 °C against MUR. The SSES bias correction trims it a little.
- By latitude (`multi`, q4), the offset against MUR is:
  - +0.32 south of 60°S
  - +0.14 at 60–40°S and 40–20°S
  - +0.24 at 20°S–0
  - +0.28 at 0–30°N

  Possible causes: clear-sky sampling (sunny days run warm in January), daytime retrievals
  in a day+night product, and, south of 60°S, the L4s filling sea-ice pixels near
  freezing where L3S has no data at all (see below).
- **Without the quality filter, the AVHRR-only product is poor** (`all`: RMS 0.44). With
  q4 it agrees with MUR (median 0.00), but only 80 cells keep enough clear-sky
  share. This is partly because the Southern Ocean single-sensor store is empty for these dates (below).

- **At L5, choosing cells moves a territory's 5-day mean by 0.1–0.5 °C**
  (MAC: exact 5.52, touching cells 5.31, majority cells 4.98). That is the same size as the
  differences between products, so L5 can't resolve the EEZ. This agrees with your
  expectation: go finer in S2, or use the exact region tables (already built).
  - The island territories are 2–3.4× smaller than their L5 cover.
  - Torres Strait is 40× smaller and has no majority cell at all.
- **Antarctic territories: L3S runs warmer than the L4s (0.07–0.22 against −0.2 to −1.1).**
  L3S only sees clear-sky open water, while MUR and OISST fill ice-covered pixels near
  freezing. A like-for-like comparison there must mask by sea-ice fraction
  (the L3S stores carry `sea_ice_fraction`), which brings in trend.ice.

## Store checks from this block

- **The Australia and Southern Ocean multi-sensor stores don't match where they overlap**
  (70–190°E, 70–27.5°S; 12.75 M pixels).
  - 4.5–5.5 M pixels are valid in both, and none are bit-identical.
  - The mean difference is 0.003 K, but single pixels differ by up to 9.5 K, and each store has pixels the other lacks (up to 0.2 M).
  - The two domains are composited separately. The composite here uses the Australia store wherever it exists.
- **The Southern Ocean single-sensor Zarr is empty for all five dates** (0 valid pixels;
  the NetCDF for 2024-01-04 has 1.2 M). Sampled across its record, some dates are full
  (1992, 1997, 2003, 2008-10, 2010-05, 2023-01) and some empty (2008-09, 2013-02,
  2024-01, 2025-03). So `single` here is the Australia window only.

## Performance

- **Reading:** gdalraster `mdim_read()` takes about 9 s for one variable's whole 5-day block of the
  Australia store (106 chunks, 183 MB). zaro `zaro_read(parallel = TRUE)` took
  121 s for the same block, with identical values.
  - All 3 variables × 5 dates × 2 stores take 64–85 s per product (`read_block()`: one request per run of consecutive time indices).
  - The first run read day by day, fetching every chunk five times: about 6 of its 13 minutes went on reads, against 2.5 of 11 minutes now.
  - The outputs of the two runs are bit-identical (60/60 tables).
- **Accumulating:** about 45 s per date, about 7.5 of the 11 minutes (48.75 M pixels × 3 variants into S2 L5 and 5 region
  sets, in plain R). This is now the slow part. The easy wins are to accumulate only domain pixels
  and to precompute the per-pixel S2 index with region membership.
